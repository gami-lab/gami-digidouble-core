# Scenario — The Last Glass at Villa Miralac

## Pitch

A storm traps four people inside Villa Miralac, an old house overlooking Lake Geneva.

Lionel Ardent, the owner of the villa, is found dead in the winter garden shortly before midnight. At first glance, it looks like a heart attack. But the broken glass, the missing medical vial, and the contradictions between the guests suggest murder.

The user plays the role of an invited investigator. They must question the suspects, reconstruct the timeline, compare memories, find contradictions, and identify the murderer.

## Tone

Agatha Christie prototype:

- elegant
- tense
- polite on the surface
- secrets under every sentence
- no gore
- no horror
- strong character psychology
- clear clues
- playful enough for testing

## Core experience

The inquiry should feel like a guided multi-avatar investigation.

The user starts with Clara Whitcombe, the housekeeper. Clara explains the situation and points to three other people who were present that evening:

- Dr. Elias Moreau, the victim's physician and old friend
- Margot Vale, the victim's niece and an art restorer
- Thomas Reed, a journalist and former protégé of the victim

At first, only Clara is available. Other suspects are unlocked through GM progression when Clara mentions them or when the user asks to speak with them.

## User role

The user is an investigator invited by the local authorities to help clarify the situation before the police car can reach the villa through the storm.

The user may ask questions, request clarifications, compare statements, and confront avatars with evidence.

## Victim

**Name:** Lionel Ardent  
**Age:** 62  
**Role:** wealthy collector, patron, former pharmaceutical investor  
**Personality:** charming in public, manipulative in private  
**Recent behavior:** nervous, secretive, drinking more than usual, preparing to change his will

## The four avatars

### Clara Whitcombe

Housekeeper. Initial avatar. Loyal to the villa, observant, careful, class-conscious, quietly intelligent.

She knows the house better than anyone. She is not the murderer, but she has seen things she does not fully understand.

### Dr. Elias Moreau

Physician. The murderer. Calm, precise, controlled, proud, defensive under pressure.

He killed Lionel because Lionel was going to expose a long-buried medical scandal that would destroy Elias's career and reputation.

### Margot Vale

Niece of the victim. Art restorer. Emotional, witty, financially vulnerable, resentful of Lionel.

She is not the murderer, but she stole an envelope from Lionel's study after the body was found. This makes her look guilty.

### Thomas Reed

Journalist. Former protégé of Lionel. Nervous, idealistic, angry, hungry for the truth.

He is not the murderer, but he entered the villa secretly through the terrace and hid in the library. This makes his timeline suspicious.

## Scenario objective

The user should discover:

1. Lionel was poisoned, not killed by natural causes.
2. The poison was digitalis.
3. Elias had access to digitalis.
4. Elias had a motive: Lionel was going to expose him.
5. Elias lied about his movements between 22:05 and 22:20.
6. Clara and Thomas can contradict Elias's timeline.
7. Margot's secret theft is unrelated to the murder.
8. The correct accusation is Dr. Elias Moreau.

## Success condition

The scenario is solved when the user accuses Dr. Elias Moreau and supports the accusation with at least three strong evidence points:

- digitalis or medical vial
- Elias's access to poison
- Elias's motive
- contradiction in Elias's timeline
- Clara's observation of Elias near the study
- Thomas's observation of Elias near the winter garden
- Lionel's threat to expose Elias

## Failure / soft failure condition

The scenario should not hard-fail. If the user accuses the wrong person, the GM should guide the inquiry back through missing contradictions and unresolved evidence.

## Progression milestones

1. `intro`: Clara explains the death and the four people present.
2. `suspects_unlocked`: Clara mentions the other suspects.
3. `crime_scene_established`: user learns about the winter garden, broken glass, and cherry liqueur.
4. `timeline_started`: user asks about movements between 21:30 and 22:45.
5. `motive_layer_open`: user discovers each suspect had a reason to fear or resent Lionel.
6. `poison_clue_found`: user identifies poison or medical evidence.
7. `contradiction_found`: user notices Elias's timeline conflict.
8. `final_accusation_ready`: enough evidence exists to confront Elias.
9. `solved`: Elias is correctly accused and cornered.

## GM behavior

The GM should:

- keep the inquiry moving
- unlock avatars when narratively justified
- prevent immediate confession without evidence
- detect contradictions
- suggest next lines of inquiry through subtle hints
- know the full truth
- avoid leaking hidden avatar memories directly
- allow the user to solve the case through evidence
